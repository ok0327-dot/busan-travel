If you've ever been to Haeundae Beach in August, you know. The sand disappears under a quilt of parasols. Korean news sometimes shows aerial photos of the beach that look like a polka-dot bedsheet, just to make the point.

Cross one street and the crowd drops by a factor of ten. That place is **Dongbaekseom**.

![Haeundae Beach](https://www.visitbusan.net/uploadImgs/files/cntnts/20191229153530528)
*Haeundae's sand — in August, more parasols than sand*

## "Island" but not really

At the western end of Haeundae Beach, one crosswalk takes you to the entrance of Dongbaekseom. The name means "camellia island," and it actually was an island once before the sand connected it to the mainland. The loop trail is 2 km — about 40 minutes at a slow pace.

The path runs along the rock shoreline, mixing wooden boardwalk with dirt trail. On one side: Gwangan Bridge. On the other: Marine City. There are two viewpoints where both come into the same frame — that's where everyone takes the photo.

## Two times to go

**Early morning (6:30–8 AM)**: Right before and during sunrise. The sea changes color minute by minute. The only other people you'll meet are joggers and elderly locals walking their dogs. Stand on the deck across from Grand Hyatt Busan when the sun comes up over Gwangan Bridge — you won't believe you're still in Haeundae.

**Right after sunset (7–8 PM)**: The moment Gwangan Bridge lights up, layered against Marine City's evening skyline reflecting in the water. Hard to take a bad photo. There are some people, but a fraction of the beach.

## Bonus: Nurimaru APEC House

Halfway around the loop sits **Nurimaru**, the building used for the 2005 APEC summit. Free entry, museum on the first floor (it's fine, not unmissable). The real gem is the **rooftop deck** — the closest you can get to the water on the whole island.

![Gwangan Bridge from Dongbaekseom](https://www.visitbusan.net/uploadImgs/files/cntnts/20191229160529389)
*The two-faced view from Dongbaek — bridge on one side, Marine City on the other*

## Honest take

Coming to Haeundae and only seeing the beach is half a visit. The Dongbaek loop takes one hour. Next time you check into your hotel, skip the sand and walk the island first. You'll understand instantly where Busan locals actually go.
